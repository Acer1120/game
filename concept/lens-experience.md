# HOLLOW VIGIL, OPEN WORLD — the player's experience, moment to moment

Lens: what it feels like to play. I walk through the opening, the first hour and a session at hour 15, then cover how exploration, encounters, cinematics and quests feel, then the risks. Everything is grounded in the build as it is now:
- the green field with full-saturation roofs and the ink line on top;
- the 20-tile 3/4 camera;
- the knight/archer kit;
- the CAMP shot layer (lens dolly, page kick, pitch/hero angle, letterbox);
- the manga callouts and the style rank;
- the wave, elite and card systems;
- the HVU units with teams, squads, passives and once-per-life ULTs.

## 0. The core idea in one sentence

**The world is a sketchbook the Vigil inks in by walking: every page holds small, readable fights staged like manga panels, and the factions (the HVU roster on real teams) are always at war with each other. You don't clear the map. You walk into fights already under way, pick a side with your first blow, and the page remembers it.**

Three rules keep every moment of play in check:
1. **Every fight fits on one screen.** The combat is tuned for 20 tiles and about 12 bodies. The open world never asks it to be anything else.
2. **Walking is looking.** Something new comes into frame about every 30 s of travel, something to do about every 90 s, and something worth telling someone about every 10 minutes.
3. **The camera tricks are rationed, not sprinkled.** At most one big shot (dolly, letterbox, cut-in) per minute of play. First-time moments get the full treatment; repeats get a short version.

## 1. The first 10 minutes

**0:00 — The opening image.** A white page with no HUD. A pen draws one lamp post in the middle of the screen, stroke by stroke, on the 10 Hz boil clock (the checklist's item 15, "title drawn stroke by stroke", becomes the game's first shot). Then it draws the knight leaning against the post, asleep. Then roofs fan outward: red, black, blue, ink first, colour flooding in a beat behind each one, exactly the look in `r1-house-full.png`. The last stroke is the flame in the lamp. The field floods green from the lamp outward. The title is hand-lettered across the sky: HOLLOW VIGIL. It holds for one second, then slides off like a torn strip. The player has control before the strip has finished leaving. Total length: about 12 s. Any key skips it.

The point: the first thing the player learns, without a word, is that **colour comes from lamps**. That is the whole game.

**0:15 — Walk.** Ashridge Junction, the town that already exists. Two WATCH guards (HVU `watch`, team 0 for now) chat in hand-lettered speech balloons that never pause you: *"Third lamp out this week."* *"Blame the Knife."* The controls legend isn't a bar over the world (checklist item 3). Each key appears once, as a small paper tag next to the knight, the first time it becomes relevant. The first is WASD. SWING appears when the player first walks near a barrel.

**1:00 — The first fight.** A fence splinters and six slimes spill into the lamp square: the scripted waves 1–2, *"the first minute teaches exactly as it always did"*. The first fight is not a lone hero, though. The two watch guards draw swords and fight **beside** you. In the first minute the player sees that units fight units, that "FROM BEHIND!" and the gold 26! are something you earn, and that the D rank letter climbs.

When the last slime pops, the killcam catches it: a telephoto snap and a page lift on the side the blade came from. That is the first killcam the player sees, and it goes on a kill that matters.

**2:30 — The hook.** A shieldbearer with two slimes teaches guard-break (the CLANG / GUARD BROKEN beat). While you fight it, a figure crosses the rooftops: **THE KNIFE**. A manga cut-in shows his eyes in a letterbox slit, lasting 0.6 s. He snuffs the lamp you woke under, and the green drains out of the square. The page is back to pencil-on-white: clean, crisp lines and no grey mud (it has to be clean; the user rejected "dirty"). Your colour was taken from you. The Watch shout *"after him!"*

**4:00 — The chase.** A short, guided run through the three streets. The Knife throws a slime pot, drops two skeletons from a cart, and then does his ULT, **SHADOWSTEP** (three dashes through you), at a point the script chooses. The player learns to dash through a telegraph. The Knife escapes over the gate at 50% HP because that is his ULT threshold. He gets away, and the player will want him.

**6:00 — The gate: the world opens.** The town gate is the portal the game already has. Before, portals were a travel plate. The first time through, they become a vista instead:
- The knight walks out onto a ridge road and the camera **eases pitch down** to the hero angle (the CAMP pitch channel that the cast and the Warden entrance already use).
- The zoom opens wide, and a **slow dolly** (VG_OPEN, but over 2.5 s, not a kick) makes the valley rush away from the knight while he stays the same size.
- Thin letterbox bars come in, and "THE VIGIL VALE" is lettered across the lower bar.

The valley below is **uninked pencil**. Three things in it are already inked, and those three are the whole invitation:
1. A **tall lamp tower** to the north. It is full colour, with its flame boiling on the 10 Hz clock. It is the next lamp.
2. A **column of smoke** to the east with drums under it: the taiko layer of the music system, played positionally. That is the orc warband's camp.
3. A **black ink blot** spreading on the horizon, the only thing on the page that isn't paper or colour: the Hollow.

The bars leave and control never stopped. The knight is already walking downhill.

**7:30 — The first roaming encounter.** On the road, a Watch rider and two pikes are fighting a pack of four skeletons. The fight is already going when you arrive. Both sides get a thin accent underline: Watch grey (#9aa5b1) and bone white. Neither has attacked you. A small hand-lettered tag floats over the fight: **"WHOSE SIDE?"** The first body you hit decides it. Almost everyone hits the skeletons, and that's fine: this is the tutorial for the game's central verb. The rider survives and salutes, and his balloon says *"Lamp tower's that way. The Knife went there too."*

**9:30 — The first lamp.** The lamp tower is guarded by a small Hold (see §5): three waves, with the existing wave banner and card system relocated to this spot. Light it and three things happen:
- The flame catches, and colour floods out from the tower across a whole page of the valley in about 3 s. The ink wash runs downhill along roads.
- A **choice card** drops: pick 1 of 3, the existing between-wave perk cards, now kept permanently as "pages" in your build.
- The lamp becomes a fast-travel point.

The player has now seen the full loop. It took ten minutes, and nothing in it was a menu.

## 2. The first hour

The first hour is where the player works out what the world is. Each beat below lands when it does for a reason:

| time | beat | what the player learns |
|---|---|---|
| 10–20 min | Free roam of the first page (about 2×2 town-sized screens). Two roaming skirmishes, a barrel yard secret, and a signpost with three **wanted posters** (THE KNIFE, THE BLADE, and a blank one with only a helmet sketched: the Warden). | Roads curve past things. Posters are quests. |
| ~20 min | **The companion archer** is found pinned down in a mill by bombers. Save her and she joins. She hands you her spare bow: **C SWAP unlocks**. The next fight is staged on purpose, with a shieldbearer wall and a sigil caster behind it, so that switching to the archer and shooting over the wall feels like a discovery rather than a tooltip. | Two classes, swapped mid-fight. |
| ~25 min | **First faction clash.** Watch versus the Orc warband at a river bridge: 8 against 8, with a CAPTAIN on one side and an ELITE ORC on the other. Opening shot: WATCH vs WARBAND as a split manga panel (see §6). | The world has politics, and they're played with a sword. |
| ~35 min | **The Blade ambush.** On a road at dusk the camera snaps to a slit letterbox. THE BLADE steps out of a treeline: a 1-v-1 duel. At 60% HP he triggers **ASTRAL TEMPEST**, and the player learns to respect named ULTs. He flees at low HP and **remembers** (§5, rivals). | Named rogues are people, not mobs. |
| ~45 min | **First blot.** You step into the ink blot's edge. The page goes monochrome, the finale `mono` uniform used as a place instead of a moment. The blot is a dungeon: the old wave run (district portals, budgeted rosters, modifiers, elites with affixes, cards) squeezed into 6–8 minutes, ending at a **blot heart**. Break the heart and the Hollow ink recedes from a region of the map. | The roguelite game lives inside the open world. |
| ~55 min | **The Warden sighting.** Leaving the blot, from the ridge you see a huge figure standing in a burned field, not moving. The shot is a dolly vertigo onto it (§6). His poster fills in with ink. You are not asked to fight him yet. | There are things out here bigger than you. |

At the end of hour one the player has: 2–3 lamps lit, 3–4 pages in the build, one rival who hates them, one faction that likes them, and an unmistakable silhouette they're afraid of. That is the "one more page" hook.

## 3. A typical 30-minute session at hour 15

The player has lit 11 of 18 lamps and swaps knight and archer freely. The Watch are allies. The Warband is split: the player spared the Warchief's son, THE LANCER. The Hollow holds the south.

- **0:00 — Open the book.** The game resumes on the **map page**, a hand-drawn sketchbook spread rather than a HUD minimap. Since the last session, **territory ink has moved**: the Warband's red hatch has crept over the Old Mill, and a new blot has bled out of the marsh. There are three torn notes pinned to it: a bounty ("TAKE THE MILL BACK — the Watch pay in lamp oil"), a rival sighting ("THE KNIFE seen at Crowfoot"), and a style contract ("FINISH FIVE WITH EXECUTIONS in one clash"). The player picks the mill. Total time on the map page: 20 s.
- **0:30 — Page flip.** Fast travel to the nearest lamp. The travel plate turns a page with a real page-turn animation, taking 1.2 s. It lands 90 s of walking from the mill.
- **1:00–3:00 — The walk is not dead time.**
  - The drums of the Warband are audible from the left before they're visible.
  - A **marginal doodle** of a crow (§4) sketches itself on the screen's right edge. Following it finds a cracked stone. **Iron Fall** on it drops into a two-room cellar holding a rare page and a note about the Hollow Knight. That takes 2 minutes.
  - Back on the road, a bat swarm plus a plaguebearer at dusk is a 40 s skirmish. The player swaps to archer for the bats: one quickdraw, one ricochet arrow, done.
- **4:00–9:00 — The mill siege (Clash + Hold hybrid).**
  - Crest the hill and the letterbox thins in: 6 Watch holding the mill against 12 Warband, with an ELITE ORC and a BERSERKER.
  - The player comes in from behind the orcs, and the style rank climbs from "FROM BEHIND!".
  - The elite orc hits 50% and roars **WARCRY**. Every orc in 7 m gets the rally tint, and the page kicks.
  - The player answers with the domain ultimate: **IRON VIGIL** freezes everything hostile in the circle. Allied Watch keep swinging inside the frozen domain, and that is the payoff of teams. It looks incredible.
  - The last orc gets a killcam. The **results card** is the existing one, re-captioned "THE MILL — RETAKEN — rank S". The mill is inked grey-blue on the map. The Watch post a guard there, and a lamp oil reward arrives.
- **10:00–20:00 — A blot dive.** The new marsh blot is a 6-wave run:
  - It uses modifiers: FOG, then SPITTERS.
  - It includes an elite **affix hunt**.
  - Cards come after waves 2 and 4.
  - It ends at a heart guarded by a NECROMANCER that uses **MASS RISE** at half health.
  - The player is at 20% HP and triggers the **last stand**. They survive, and they will tell someone about it.
  - The blot recedes and the marsh inks green.
- **21:00–27:00 — The rival.** The Knife note pulls the player to Crowfoot. THE KNIFE is sitting on a roof and has **learned**: he opens with a smoke bomb because the player kept parrying him last time (the rival memory in §5). A 2-minute duel ends in an EXECUTE prompt: execute or spare. The player spares him. A 3-panel strip slides in (§7). He'll show up later.
- **28:00 — Bank the ink at a lamp.** The session's unbanked style score (see §8, risks) is written into the lamp. There's a short **session page**, the marks wall showing what you stamped today, and the player can stop at a clean stopping point.

That's four kinds of play (exploration, clash, run and duel) in 30 minutes, and none of them lasted longer than 10 minutes.

## 4. Exploration: why walk over that hill

A top-down 3/4 camera can't see the horizon, and that is the core problem for exploration. The design answers it in five ways.

**4.1 Uninked land is the invitation.** Anything the player hasn't reached is **pencil on white**: crisp lines and no colour. When you arrive, it inks and colours in 0.4 s around you, and the colour stays. Lighting a lamp floods the whole page. The post pass already separates *wash* from *ink* (`vUv` versus `vUv+boil`), and there is already a CLARITY dial plus named floor tint and saturation constants. An "inked" mask per world cell is one more multiplier on the wash. Players are completionists about colour: an uncoloured patch on a coloured map itches, the way fog-of-war does, but it is more beautiful.

*Guard (the user's words on Sep 9): "too faded, dirty, muddy".* The uninked state must be **clean bright paper with black line**, never grey. It works as contrast, so the colour pops harder when it arrives. The player's surroundings are coloured within 4 tiles immediately, so a fight is never fought in pencil.

**4.2 Marginalia instead of a minimap.** The edge of the screen is the margin of the sketchbook. Points of interest outside the frame are doodled into the margin on the side they're on, small and boiling on the shared clock:
- a flame for an unlit lamp;
- a smoke curl for a faction camp;
- a skull for a boss;
- a question mark for a secret the player has heard a rumour about;
- crossed swords for a clash **in progress**. These pulse, because the fight is happening now.

Doodles grow as you approach and are rubbed out when you arrive. No compass bar and no minimap: the HUD stays clean, which matters a lot given checklist items 1–4.

**4.3 Sound before sight.** The procedural WebAudio plays positionally: taiko from a clash, enemy voices, the necromancer's chant, a bell at a lamp. You hear a war about 40 tiles off, then the doodle appears, then the smoke, and then you crest the ridge. That is a four-step reveal and the player drove all of it.

**4.4 Landmarks tall enough to break the frame.** Lamp towers, the Warden's burned oak, the Hollow's black spire and orc totems all have **tall billboards whose tops poke into view** long before their bases, drawn with heavier ink. Ridges and bridges lower the camera pitch slightly, a mild hero angle, so the far landmarks show. The player learns: **walk to high ground to look.**

**4.5 Secrets are ability-shaped.** The kit is already rich. Each ability doubles as a key in the world, and none is a hard gate on the critical path:

| ability | world use |
|---|---|
| **Iron Fall** | Breaks cracked stones and drops into cellars and caves. |
| **Grapple / hook** | Crosses gorges to ink-post anchors, and yanks down loose rope bridges. |
| **Bomb arrow** | Collapses boarded walls and rockfalls. |
| **Ricochet arrow** | Rings bells round a corner, and hits two switches in one shot. |
| **Ground sigil** | Drawn in stone circles, opens barrow doors. The sigil is a drawing, and drawings are magic in this world. |
| **Scribble decoy** | Lures a sleeping werebear off a chest, or pulls a patrol away from a camp. |
| **Crescent wave** | Cuts reeds and bramble walls. |
| **Ink cyclone** | Clears ink puddles, the Hollow's hazard. |

**4.6 The paper's edges: the torn corner.** The world ends at the **edge of the page**: a torn paper edge, with a desk-coloured void beyond it. It isn't an invisible wall, so it reads as an honest boundary. Some corners are **dog-eared**. Iron Fall on a dog-ear triggers the page tilt at full strength: the page lifts and flips, and you land on the **back of the page**. That is a reversed drawing, the impact-frame `invert` look held as a place: ink-white lines on a dark page. The back holds the hardest secrets, the Hollow Knight's lair, and pencilled notes from "whoever drew this world". This is the game's best secret and the one players will tell each other about.

**4.7 Traversal feel.** Out of combat, the knight's run ramps to a jog after 1.5 s of steady movement. Dash, stinger and hook chain into a **momentum string** that keeps speed. Style rank doesn't decay while travelling, so arriving at a fight mid-dash with a stinger is the fast *and* stylish opener. Horses (the `rider` sheet) are an hour-20 luxury, not a need. The world is small enough on foot (§8.1).

## 5. Encounters in an open world, tuned for tight fights

**5.1 The panel leash: how a fight holds its shape without walls.** When you engage, the game inks a faint **manga panel border** around the fight: a rounded rectangle about one camera frame wide (roughly 20 × 12 tiles), drawn with the same wobble helpers as the telegraphs. The border is the fight:
- Hostiles don't pursue beyond it. They return to their post after 3 s outside it, with a *"tch"* balloon.
- New bodies from outside wait at the gutter to enter (the existing cap/budget logic). They never pile in off-screen.
- Walk out of the border and it rubs out. The fight has ended, not been lost.
- Some set pieces **lock** the border with a heavier ink line: holds, boss fights and blot hearts.

Tight, readable fights are guaranteed by construction, and a fight is never lost to a body that's off-screen.

**5.2 Encounter types, the vocabulary of the world:**

| type | bodies | length | from existing systems | the feeling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Skirmish** | 3–6, one squad | 30–60 s | HVU squads, `aggro`, spacing rings | Rhythm, style practice, a speed bump on the road. |
| **Clash** | 2 sides, 8–20 total | 2–4 min | teams, `pickTarget`, rally/volley/warcry ULTs | Walking into a war and choosing. |
| **Hold** | waves, 3–5 | 3–6 min | the whole wave system, banner, cards, modifiers | Defend a lamp or a village: the original game, placed in the world. |
| **Blot run** | wave run + heart | 6–10 min | districts, budgeted rosters, elites/affixes, bounties, cards | Roguelite stakes inside the world. |
| **Hunt / Rival** | 1 named plus 0–3 | 1–3 min | named rogues, ULTs, elite affixes | A duel with a person. |
| **Boss** | 1 plus adds | 3–6 min | Warden, Hollow Knight, Warchief (RAMPAGE) | A page-level event. |

**5.3 Clashes: picking a side.** This is the most important new experience, and it is built almost entirely out of the units' team AI.
- Factions come from the roster:
  - **The Watch**: watch, captain, lancer, blade, pike, swordsman, knight, archer, rider.
  - **The Warband**: orc, elite orc, iron orc, axeman, berserker, warchief.
  - **The Hollow**: skeletons, iron and great skeleton, skeleton archer, necro, revenant, warlock, plaguebearer, bats.
  - **The Choir**: priest, templar, hierophant, mesmer, wizard.
  - **The Wild**: slimes, werewolf, werebear. The Wild are hostile to everyone, and they are the third-party chaos.
- When the player enters a clash, both sides get their accent underline and nobody targets the player (`P.team=-1`, the value HVU already honours for "leave me alone").
- **Your first damaging blow picks the side.** A two-sided cut-in stamps it: `INK SIDES WITH THE WATCH`. The side you hit flips to hostile, the other side to allied (team 0). Accidental hits on allies get a 1.5 s grace with a *"hey!"* balloon, so one stray ricochet doesn't flip a war.
- **Or pick neither.** Stand back and let them grind each other down, then clean up the weakened winner for more loot and less honour. The factions notice (§5.5).
- **Reading a clash at scale:**
  - only bodies within the panel run full sim at full fidelity;
  - the rest resolve as a **tally skirmish**: pencil tally marks in the margin, with bodies dying off-frame at a scripted rate;
  - the front line always stays inside the frame.
- **The leader rule:** every clash has one body with a named ULT (captain, elite orc, necro, priest). Its ULT is the clash's climax: LAST STAND volleys, WARCRY, MASS RISE, SANCTUARY. Killing the leader breaks the side into a rout (they flee at the edge of the panel). The killcam goes on the leader, not the last body.

**5.4 Roaming groups** move along roads on a timetable: patrols by day, Hollow at night (NIGHTFALL becomes a real time of day, and lamps matter because Hollow bodies won't enter a lit radius). They meet each other, so **clashes happen on their own** where roads cross. The player's map doodle of crossed swords is what makes those encounters findable.

**5.5 Rivals and faction memory, so fights aren't anonymous.**
- The four named rogues (KNIFE, BLADE, LANCER, WATCH) plus later the headhunter and warchief are **rivals**. Each keeps a tiny memory: what killed them last, what the player spammed, spared or executed.
- They come back with one counter-trait from their own move set: the Knife opens with smoke, the Lancer throws from range if you always parried, the Blade triggers his ULT earlier.
- **Spare** at the execute prompt and they may show up later as an ally, team 0 for one fight.
- Factions keep a single ink stamp count per player: friendly, neutral or hunted. At *hunted*, that faction's patrols ambush you.

## 6. The cinematic toolbox, used for open-world moments

The shot layer already exists: lens dolly (`lensKick`, VG_OPEN/VG_EXEC), page tilt (`pageKick`), the pitch and hero angle, the letterbox (`LB`, with `body.cine` sliding the HUD out), the killcam, manga cut-ins and the domain. The open world gives each tool a **fixed meaning**, so the player learns the camera's grammar:

| tool | fixed meaning in the open world | moment |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow dolly out + pitch down + thin letterbox** | *"Look how big this is."* | **Vista**: first arrival on a ridge overlooking a new page. Control is kept. The region name is hand-lettered in the bottom bar. First time only; afterwards just the lettering. |
| **Dolly vertigo IN (subject frozen, background rushing)** | *"That is dangerous."* | **Boss discovery**: the Warden in his burned field, or the Hollow Knight's silhouette on the back of the page. 1.5 s, control kept, and the poster inks in. |
| **Split-panel cut-in (two faces, a gutter between)** | *"Choose."* | **Clash start**: WATCH vs WARBAND. Then the SIDE CHOSEN stamp. |
| **Letterbox slit (very thin bars) + an eyes cut-in** | *"Someone is hunting you."* | **Rival ambush**: the Blade steps out of the trees. |
| **Page tilt at full strength + page turn** | *"You are going somewhere else."* | Fast travel, entering a blot, flipping to the back of the page, going into a cellar. |
| **Killcam (telephoto snap + page lift)** | *"That one mattered."* | The **leader** of a clash, a rival's execute, a blot heart breaking, the last body of a Hold. Never on a random skirmish kill. |
| **Domain ultimate (IRON VIGIL / HOLLOW VOLLEY)** | *"I end this."* | Its world role: the domain **freezes only hostile teams**, so allies keep fighting inside it. Cast in a clash, it is the player stopping a war. Cast at a blot heart, the domain ring *inks the ground clean* as it closes, and the heart can only be broken inside a domain. |
| **Colour flood (the post pass wash)** | *"This is yours now."* | Lighting a lamp, retaking the mill, cleansing a blot. The world's reward is visual. |
| **The impact frame / `mono`** | *"The Hollow."* | Blot interiors and the back of the page use the reversed drawing as a place. |

**The shot budget.** A tiny director allows one "big" shot (anything letterboxed or dollied) per 60 s, with a priority order: boss > rival > clash > vista > killcam. It also enforces "first time full, repeats short". The user already finds the current banners and bands "not satisfying" and overprinted (checklist items 6–8). An open world multiplies that problem unless something says no.

## 7. Quests told without breaking pace: the manga/paper UI

- **No dialogue boxes and no pausing, ever, outside the three boss intros.** NPCs speak in **in-world speech balloons** above their heads while you keep walking. A balloon is drawn with the wobble helpers and held for about 2.5 s. They're short: 8 words or fewer.
- **Story beats are 3-panel strips.** A strip slides into the top-left third of the screen like a torn page taped on: panel one the setup, panel two the face, panel three the line. It plays for about 4 s and you keep control. Then it folds away into the **sketchbook** (the quest log), where it can be reread. Strips are drawn from the sprites already in the game, at a big scale with the ink pass, so they cost no new art.
- **Quest objects are paper, pinned to things in the world:**
  - **Wanted posters** on signposts: rivals and bosses. The portrait inks in as you learn about them, blank helmet to full portrait.
  - **Torn notes** on lamp notice boards: bounties. The existing BOUNTIES (TAKE NO HIT, FINISH TWO, CLEAR IN 40s) become *style contracts* attached to places ("finish two by execution at the bridge clash").
  - **Pencil question marks** on the map: rumours from balloons.
- **Tracking** is a single marginal doodle (§4.2) of the active quest's target, drawn with a heavier ink line. That's all.
- **Completion** is a stamp, not a toast: a red ink stamp lands on the note or poster with a taiko hit, and the note flies into the sketchbook. The existing **marks wall** becomes the sketchbook's back pages, and **results cards** caption places ("THE MILL — RETAKEN — S").
- **Main story in one line:** the Hollow is spilling ink across the valley and putting out the lamps. The Warden guards the source, the Hollow Knight *is* the source, and the Knife has been stealing flames for someone. Every main beat is a place you can see from a ridge.

## 8. The biggest risks to fun, and how the design avoids each

**8.1 Travel time and tedium.** *The risk:* an open world made of empty green between fights. The combat is the fun; walking isn't.
- **Small and dense.** The world is about 18 pages (lamp regions). Each page is about 2×2 screens of today's town, and the valley is roughly 6×6 pages. Crossing a page on foot takes 60–90 s; crossing the valley takes about 8 minutes, and nobody ever has to.
- **Pacing grid.** Look every 30 s, do every 90 s, tell every 10 min. This is checked with a debug overlay that marks "nothing within 25 tiles" dead zones on the map, so they can be filled or cut.
- Fast travel between lit lamps as a 1.2 s page turn.
- The momentum string (dash, stinger, hook) makes travel itself a skill toy.
- Roads are drawn to curve past secrets.
- Skirmishes are opt-in by distance: patrols' aggro radius is honest and visible, a faint spacing ring at their feet, so you can route around them.

**8.2 Samey fights.** *The risk:* the 40th skirmish against the same watch squad.
- **Combinatorics that already exist:**
  - 40 unit kinds in 5 factions;
  - squads with flanking;
  - wave modifiers as time of day and weather (FOG, NIGHTFALL, FRENZY);
  - elite affixes;
  - named ULTs as fight climaxes.
- **Third parties.** Clashes can be joined by the Wild (a werebear wanders in) or by the Hollow at night. A three-way fight is never the same twice.
- **Terrain inside the panel:** a bridge (a choke), a mill (a hold), a lamp radius (Hollow can't enter), ink puddles (a hazard), fences (Iron Fall shockwaves break them).
- **Your side changes the fight.** The same bridge played with the Watch (shield wall ahead of you) and against them (the captain's volleys) is two different fights.
- **Rivals adapt** (§5.5).
- **Style contracts** ask for a different way to play the same fight.

**8.3 Readability at scale.** *The risk:* 20 bodies, two teams, ink VFX, damage numbers and callouts turn into mud. The user has already flagged ink density and overprinted UI (checklist items 1, 2, 7, 9, 10).
- The **panel leash** (§5.1) limits the fight to one frame.
- **A cap on full-sim bodies near the player** (about 14 active); the rest are tally-resolved (§5.3).
- **Team read at a glance:** allies get a thin accent underline and no damage numbers (only your hits print). Enemies get the red telegraph rings. Only **one** manga callout stream, the player's; ally ULTs print as small balloons, not stamps.
- **VFX priority:** player ink first, then enemy telegraphs, then ally effects at half opacity.
- **The shot budget** (§6) stops cut-ins stacking.

**8.4 Losing the tension of the run.** *The risk:* an open world makes death cheap and the roguelite spine (cards, waves, rank) goes soft.
- **Blot runs keep real stakes:** die inside and the run's cards are lost, and the blot regrows.
- **In the open world, style score is "unbanked ink".** It builds as you fight and is banked at a lamp. Die and you wake at the last lamp, and the unbanked ink is left as a **blot where you fell**. Walk back and you can reclaim it: a light souls-style retrieval that makes a long walk between lamps tense.
- **The last stand** remains the second-chance moment.

**8.5 Number creep.** *The risk:* XP levels turn the combat into bigger numbers, which kills the feel.
- **No character levels.** Progression is pages (perk cards kept at lamps, with a slot limit so builds are choices), kit unlocks (abilities found at landmarks), marks, and faction standing.
- Enemy HP scales by **region and faction**, not by player level, so a skeleton is always a skeleton, and going back to the first page makes you feel strong.

**8.6 Cinematic fatigue and lost control.** *The risk:* the camera tricks become the thing players skip.
- Control is kept in every shot except three boss intros.
- First time full, repeats short.
- A setting to reduce shots.
- A shot never plays in the middle of a panel fight unless it's the killcam on the leader.

**8.7 Wandering with no goal.** *The risk:* at hour 3 a player doesn't know what they want.
- There are always **three visible pulls**: the nearest unlit lamp (flame doodle), the loudest clash (crossed swords), and a poster target.
- The map page shows territory changing between sessions, which gives a reason to go back to places.

## 9. The three moments a player tells a friend about
